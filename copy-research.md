# Copy research notes

## Pangram

Source: https://www.pangram.com/

Observed positioning: Pangram leads with a direct product promise around detecting AI-generated content and preserving human authorship. Its interactive hero places the text input beside the headline, offers concrete classification modes such as Human, ChatGPT, and AI + Human, and explains the workflow as four steps: input text, scan for AI content, view results, and access past records. It also frames transparency as a product principle and distinguishes fully AI-generated text from AI-assisted editing.

Reusable lesson: lead with the user outcome, put the core action close to the headline, use concrete result categories, and describe the product workflow in plain verbs.

## GPTZero

Source: https://gptzero.me/

Observed positioning: GPTZero uses the headline “AI detector made to Preserve What’s Human.” Its supporting copy explains the supported models and frames the purpose as helping readers understand writing. The interactive hero offers clear source categories such as ChatGPT, Claude, Human, AI + Human, Polished by AI, and Paraphrased by AI, with a direct Scan action. Down-page content uses user-centered outcome blocks such as “Scan top AI models,” “Verify real writing,” “Connect your classroom,” and “Improve with AI Tutor.” Its FAQ explicitly includes detector limitations and accuracy.

Reusable lesson: pair a human-centered promise with concrete scan states, use action-led benefit language, and make limitations part of the product story rather than hiding them.

## Editorial direction for this project

Use a confident but evidence-led voice. Replace vague phrases such as “Find the signal behind the writing,” “Your text, explained,” and “What influenced the score” with clearer copy that tells users what to do and what the result means. Prefer concrete verbs—paste, scan, review, compare, inspect—and outcome language—sentence evidence, writing patterns, reviewable estimate. Avoid unsupported absolute accuracy claims and avoid repetitive privacy slogans; keep the factual privacy explanation in the FAQ and use lighter product language in the hero and controls.

## Grammarly

Source: https://www.grammarly.com/ai-detector

Observed positioning: Grammarly leads with a quality-oriented promise and explains the result as how much of the work “appears to be AI-generated.” Its primary action is “Scan for AI,” while the input supports paste, upload, and sample text. It organizes benefits around useful outcomes such as authentic authorship, explanations for flagged phrases, and reduced false positives.

Reusable lesson: describe the result as an estimate about the text, keep the action literal, and connect detection to the user’s next review step.

## Originality.ai

Source: https://originality.ai/

Observed positioning: Originality.ai leads with a concise “Accurate AI Detector” promise, then explains a three-step workflow: add text, scan, review results. Its most responsible guidance says to use an AI score as one signal rather than a final decision and to keep a human reviewer in the loop. It also makes false positives visible as a limitation rather than presenting detection as proof.

Reusable lesson: make the workflow explicit, state the interpretation boundary near the result, and keep accuracy claims tied to a named study or dataset instead of presenting them as universal performance.
